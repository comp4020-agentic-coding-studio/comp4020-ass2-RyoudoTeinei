# Process overview

## What I built

I built How to Leave, a twelve-week course that teaches ordinary subscribers to identify the biller, understand their commitment, follow the appropriate cancellation route and verify the result. Using a coding agent, I developed the lectures, workshops, assessments, sourced platform cases and practice lab around that practical learning sequence.

## How I directed the work

I began by reading the Assignment 2 requirements with the agent and separating fixed requirements from course-design decisions. The site needed twelve dated teaching weeks, assessment weights totalling 100 percent, a linked lecture deck and traceable process evidence. Inspecting the starter established the technical boundary: Astro, the SlopU identity, four content collections and the generated API were already fixed. I treated those as constraints on the plan.

I divided the work into curriculum content, assessment, platform references, the practice lab, presentation and verification. I mapped those modules onto the existing project: teaching records in `src/content/`, routes in `src/pages/`, shared layouts and components, a source register in `src/data/`, lab behaviour in `src/lib/`, slides in `src/decks/` and checks in `spec/`. Markdown and MDX carried the teaching material; TypeScript handled the interactive practice. This structure kept content, behaviour and presentation independently understandable.

After the agent proposed a plan, I reviewed the audience, modules and boundaries. Its initial direction emphasised designing cancellation interfaces. I redirected the course towards teaching consumers how to cancel, and asked for real examples, particularly Adobe. I wanted a good course to build a reusable skill through progressively harder cases. That meant replacing interface-prototype assessments with an inventory, a field guide and an unfamiliar-case practical. I also bounded the exercises to fictional accounts and required platform instructions to carry sources, dates and applicability. These decisions became working rules in CLAUDE.md and curriculum checks in [a4f0034](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RyoudoTeinei/commit/a4f0034).

The reviewed plan became my to-do list: establish course records and constraints, complete the weekly content and assessments, build the shared interface, implement the lab, then verify the result. I had the agent translate those tasks into implementation steps and acceptance conditions. Completion required aligned lectures and workshops, consistent dates and weights, scoped case sources, working links and a lab that could not report success before verification. The curriculum tests and implementation in [9079970](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RyoudoTeinei/commit/9079970) make those conditions inspectable.

The later corrections followed those boundaries. Adobe's US terms, the proposed settlement announcement and the company's response remain distinguishable in the source register. In the lab, a deliberate timeout requires learners to check the resulting account state. I had the agent preserve that condition, including reset behaviour, in `spec/exit-lab.test.ts` in [9079970](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RyoudoTeinei/commit/9079970). Browser review then prompted a clearer proposed-result label and more explicit platform instructions.

Finally, verification exposed phone text that was too small in the fixed-size deck. The correction in [4fd56e2](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RyoudoTeinei/commit/4fd56e2) enlarged mobile typography and added unscaled navigation controls. The agent checked the 39-page build, internal links and accessibility requirements; twelve tests passed, alongside browser checks at both marking viewports. I left curriculum coherence and fair interpretation of sources to editorial judgement, because passing structural checks cannot establish either.
