# Process overview

## What I built

I built How to Leave, a twelve-week introductory course about cancelling subscriptions, using a coding agent. I wanted everyday users to learn how to identify the biller, read the commitment, choose an appropriate route and verify the result. Five lessons use Apple, Google Play, Adobe and Netflix as sourced cases. I connected these lessons to workshops, three assessments, a source register and an optional practice lab, while retaining the provided SlopU identity, Astro platform, content collections and generated API.

## How the course took shape

The initial proposal approached cancellation as an interface-design problem. I redirected it towards teaching consumers how to act, with real platform examples. That changed both the audience and the assessed work: I chose an inventory, a cancellation field guide and an unfamiliar-case response instead of interface prototypes. I carried that decision into the agent's working rules in CLAUDE.md and the curriculum checks before developing the content. The first checkpoint, [a4f0034](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RyoudoTeinei/commit/a4f0034), records those constraints and a Windows installation correction that preserves the starter's secret-scanning hook.

I structured the teaching sequence around identifying subscriptions and commitments, working through platform cases, then checking results and resolving problems. Each lecture has observable learning outcomes and a linked workshop producing an annotated record or explanation. I made the three assessments reuse those skills at weeks four, eight and twelve, with weights of 25, 30 and 45 percent. In [9079970](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RyoudoTeinei/commit/9079970), these dates and weights live in the course collections, keeping the website and catalogue API consistent.

I made source handling part of the teaching method. The Adobe material distinguishes US plan terms, the government's proposed settlement announcement and the company's response, with scope and verification dates beside the instructions. I used fictional receipts so every required exercise could be completed without buying a service or revealing personal account details. I encoded these decisions as writing rules and structured source metadata. I kept the limits of automation explicit: checks can establish that metadata is present, but assessing whether an explanation fairly represents a source requires human judgement.

I used the lab to make the distinction between an attempt and an outcome tangible. A request deliberately times out; learners must inspect the simulated account state before receiving a completion record. I had the agent test premature verification and the risk of a late timeout affecting a reset case. Browser review also prompted an explicit label on the pre-request result preview, keeping a proposed outcome distinct from a completed cancellation.

I treated usability verification as part of completing the teaching materials. The review in [4fd56e2](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-RyoudoTeinei/commit/4fd56e2) added concise action sequences to the platform lessons and corrected a problem compilation missed: the fixed-size deck made phone text too small. Larger mobile typography and unscaled navigation controls improved readability without changing the renderer. I had the agent check the 39-page build, internal links and automated accessibility requirements; twelve tests passed. Browser checks at 1920 by 1080 and 390 by 844 exercised navigation, search, the lab and slides. I kept curriculum coherence and the fairness of explanations outside those automated checks, where they require editorial judgement.
